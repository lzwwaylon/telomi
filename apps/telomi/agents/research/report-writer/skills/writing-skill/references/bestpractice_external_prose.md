# Research Report Prose Guide

This is editorial guidance for the Writer Root, not a fixed template or acceptance checklist.

## Reader path

- Lead with the most important judgment, then show how the evidence changes or supports it.
- Introduce concepts in the order action -> difference -> impact -> name. Let the reader see the change before naming the abstraction.
- Move one main object or causal change per paragraph without compressing the report into one-sentence outline fragments.
- Keep necessary context. Low cognitive load means easy causal tracking, not simply fewer words.

## Structure

- Derive Sections from the question and evidence. Do not dump Sources one by one or impose a reusable background-method-results template.
- Timelines, matrices, and layered frameworks may help planning, but keep only a framework that materially reduces reading effort.
- Give repeated models, institutions, metrics, and architecture explanations one primary Section. Use short cross-references elsewhere.
- Put limitations near the conclusions they qualify rather than collecting every caveat at the end.

## Voice

- Write like a practitioner sharing an evidence-corrected judgment, not a lecturer, catalog, or marketing page.
- Ground evaluation in concrete objects, actions, and consequences. Avoid hollow importance labels and inflated abstractions.
- Avoid meta narration such as “next we analyze” or “the following three points.” Let the content make the transition.
- Preserve real disagreement and non-comparability instead of smoothing it away for fluency.

## Final-edit questions

1. Does the opening answer the user's real question?
2. Does every Section own a distinct responsibility?
3. If framework labels disappear, do concrete facts and causal links still carry the report?
4. Does every conclusion remain inside the frozen Notes and citation boundary?
